Same format as Topic Matrix 1. One block per category:
- **~Types**: approximate count of distinct question shapes
- **Handling**: FULL / PARTIAL / SOFT DEFLECT / HARD DEFLECT
- **How much**: length + what to include
- **Tone / deflect line**: how it sounds when not answering fully

Same universal rule applies: acknowledge → give the real reason (not
"policy") → offer the call/office when deflecting.

### 1. Weather, Culture & Local Language — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL
- **How much**: Honest, light — climate by country, local language exists
  but daily life/university runs in English, cultural norms Indian students
  adapt to easily.
- **Tone**: Casual, reassuring.

### 2. Agent / Consultant Selection — ~6–10 types
- **Handling**: FULL (on what makes a good consultant) / never badmouth a
  named competitor.
- **How much**: General checklist (transparent fees, verifiable office,
  willing to answer hard questions, doesn't pressure). Let the lead compare
  us against that checklist rather than trashing others.
- **Tone**: Confident, non-defensive.

### 3. Scams, Red Flags & Fraud Prevention — ~8–12 types
- **Handling**: FULL — major trust-building set-piece, same tier as FMGE.
- **How much**: Real red flags (no written agreement, pressure to pay before
  seeing documents, no verifiable office/counselor, vague on NMC criteria).
  Answer generously — an informed lead trusts us more, not less.
- **Tone**: Protective, "we want you to know this whoever you go with."

### 4. University Comparison — ~6–10 types
- **Handling**: SOFT DEFLECT
- **How much**: General comparison framework only (cost tier, hospital
  access, community size) — never declare a winner between two named
  universities in chat.
- **Tone / deflect**: "Comparing universities properly needs your specific
  score and priorities — that's exactly what the call maps out."

### 5. Country Comparison — ~6–10 types
- **Handling**: PARTIAL
- **How much**: Neutral distinguishing factors between named countries
  (cost tier, language, Indian community size, safety profile). No verdict.
- **Tone / deflect**: "Depends what matters most to you — cost, safety,
  community, or return path. Worth mapping that out on the call."

### 6. India MBBS vs MBBS Abroad — ~6–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL — this is the core value proposition.
- **How much**: Full cost gap comparison (₹80L–1.2Cr India private vs. the far
  lower economical route abroad — the ₹27–35L countries), FMGE path explained
  honestly. Use freely.
- **Tone**: Confident, this is the pitch.

### 7. Private MBBS India vs MBBS Abroad — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: PARTIAL
- **How much**: Same cost-gap framing as above, but flag that exact private
  college fees in India vary a lot by state/college — give the range, not a
  specific college's number.
- **Tone / deflect**: "Private India fees really range a lot college to
  college — happy to help you compare properly against your specific
  options on a call."

### 8. Admission Timeline & Intake — ~6–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL
- **How much**: Intake months; the ~28-day process timeline is specific to UG
  MBBS students applying to study abroad — say it with that scope. Real
  urgency already exists in the calendar — no need to manufacture it.
- **Tone**: Time-aware, practical.

### 9. Payment, Refunds & Financial Policies — ~6–10 types
- **Handling**: HARD DEFLECT
- **How much**: Nothing specific. Payment schedules and refund terms are
  contractual — a bot stating them wrong creates real liability. This is a
  new rule not yet in the build plan's non-negotiables — worth adding
  alongside the financing/scholarship deflect.
- **Tone / deflect**: "That's exactly the kind of thing that needs to be in
  writing and explained properly, not typed out over chat — the counselor
  covers this clearly on the call."

### 10. Pre-Departure Support — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL
- **How much**: Orientation session, packing guidance, SIM/documentation
  checklist, visa paperwork help — describe generally and warmly.
- **Tone**: Reassuring, "we don't just admit you and disappear."

### 11. Post-Arrival Support — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL — major parent-trust category, same tier as Travel &
  Departure in Matrix 1.
- **How much**: Airport pickup, local senior/buddy system, settling-in help.
  Give freely.
- **Tone**: Warm, especially effective for parent-voice messages.

### 12. Emergency & Student Support — ~6–10 types
- **Handling**: FULL, but careful — no overpromising.
- **How much**: Describe the support structure honestly (helpline, local
  contact, hospital access) without guaranteeing outcomes in a crisis.
- **Tone**: Calm, factual, protective — never dismissive of the fear.

### 13. Transfer / Migration Between Universities — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL — important factual/eligibility info.
- **How much**: State clearly that switching mid-course generally forfeits
  the "no transfer" condition required for Indian recognition (one of the
  6 NMC/FMGE criteria) — this is real, protective information.
- **Tone**: Direct, "this is exactly the kind of thing that trips students
  up, glad you asked."

### 14. Gap Year / Dropper Students — ~6–10 types
- **Handling**: FULL for general reassurance, PARTIAL on specific edge cases
  (age limits, multiple-attempt scoring).
- **How much**: General reassurance that gap years/droppers are common and
  fine; specific eligibility edge cases → confirm on call.
- **Tone / deflect on edge cases**: "Your specific case is worth checking
  properly — happy to confirm exactly where you stand on a quick call."

### 15. Special Student Cases — ~4–8 types
- **Handling**: SOFT DEFLECT
- **How much**: Acknowledge the situation is genuinely unique, don't attempt
  a generalized answer.
- **Tone / deflect**: "That's a case worth looking at properly rather than
  guessing over chat — let's get you on with the counselor directly."

### 16. Parent–Student Decision Making — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL (validation) + bridge to joint call for resolution
- **How much**: Validate that disagreement is normal for a decision this
  big; don't try to mediate the actual disagreement in chat.
- **Tone**: Empathetic, non-judgmental of either side. "Would a joint call
  with both of you help sort this out together?"

### 17. Personalized Profile Assessment — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: SOFT DEFLECT — but treat as a hot signal, not a brush-off.
- **How much**: "Can't properly assess a profile in chat" + immediate
  assumptive-close attempt, since this request itself signals strong
  intent.
- **Tone / deflect**: "This is exactly what the call is for — 15 minutes and
  the counselor gives you a real, specific read on your options. Wednesday
  11am or would tomorrow work better?"

### 18. Counseling Process — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL — reduces booking friction, so answer generously.
- **How much**: What happens on the call, how long, is it free, who's on it.
  This directly supports the close.
- **Tone**: Warm, low-pressure, "no obligation, just a real conversation."

### 19. Company & Counselor Information — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL, but only with real facts Hamza has provided — nothing
  invented.
- **How much**: Name, experience, credentials — as given. *(Dependency:
  this needs actual counselor/company details before it can be answered
  well — not yet supplied in this conversation.)*
- **Tone**: Confident, factual, no exaggeration.

### 20. Success Stories & Student Experiences — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL, only with real, provided material — never fabricate a
  specific story, name, or outcome.
- **How much**: If real anonymized examples exist, share generously — huge
  trust builder. If none provided yet, stay generic ("many families have
  gone through this with us") rather than inventing detail.
- **Tone**: Warm, credible, specific when real material exists.

### 21. Testimonials & Social Proof — ~4–6 types
- **Handling**: FULL, same fabrication rule as above.
- **How much**: Real quotes/ratings if supplied; otherwise general
  reassurance only.
- **Tone**: Confident but honest — no invented five-star quotes.

### 22. Common Myths & Misconceptions — ~6–10 types
- **Handling**: FULL — fear-defusing set-piece, same tier as FMGE.
- **How much**: Correct the myth directly and clearly (e.g. "MBBS abroad
  degrees aren't recognized" → false, if the 6 NMC criteria are met).
- **Tone**: Confident, myth-busting, never condescending about the question.

### 23. Mistakes Students Make — ~6–10 types
- **Handling**: FULL
- **How much**: Real generalized patterns (not specific names) — e.g. not
  verifying NMC criteria before paying, choosing on price alone, skipping
  the written agreement. Doubles as a subtle case for needing guidance.
- **Tone**: Honest, protective, "we see this go wrong more than it should."

### 24. Frequently Asked Questions — meta-category
- **Handling**: Not a distinct answer type — route to whichever specific
  category the actual question falls under (categories 1–23 or Matrix 1).
- **Note for build**: don't build a separate "FAQ" answer path — this
  should just be how the KB is indexed/searched, not a behavior all its own.

### 25. High-Intent Admission Questions — ~5–8 types
- **Handling**: FULL + always paired with a direct/assumptive close
- **How much**: These are late-funnel buying signals ("how do I pay the
  fee," "can we start documents now," "when can we begin"). Answer plainly
  and immediately offer to book, regardless of what message number this is.
- **Tone**: Match their energy — if they sound ready, act ready. "Let's get
  this moving — I can set up a call today or tomorrow, which works?"

## New rule surfaced by this matrix — NOW ADOPTED

**Never state specific payment schedules or refund/cancellation terms in
chat** — these are contractual and a bot error here creates real liability,
same severity tier as the financing and admission-guarantee rules already in
the build plan. **Adopted 2026-09-08 as build-plan §2 rule 7**, enforced as a
deterministic Tier-1 Response Guard block (`payment_terms_disclosure`).

## Combination patterns

| Combo | What they're really asking | Handle by |
|---|---|---|
| Scams + Agent selection + Payment/refunds | "How do I not get burned on money?" | Full scam education, hard-deflect the payment specifics, redirect to written agreement via counselor |
| Country comparison + University comparison + India vs. Abroad | Comparison shopping, early-stage | General frameworks only, no verdicts, pivot to personalized comparison call |
| Gap year + NEET score + Special case | Edge-case eligibility check | General reassurance, flag individual case needs confirming on call |
| Success stories + Testimonials + Company info | Skeptical lead (often parent) doing due diligence | Full honest answers, real material only, no fabrication |
| Personalized profile + High-intent admission + Counseling process | Hot buying signal cluster | Strong direct/assumptive close, don't over-explain |
| Weather + Culture + Post-arrival support | Comfort-seeking, usually excited/nervous student | Full warm answers, no deflection needed |
| Parent-student decision + Emergency support + Safety (Matrix 1) | Parent-voice anxiety cluster | Validate, answer safety/emergency honestly, offer joint call |
| Mistakes + Myths + Red flags | Building a mental checklist before deciding | Full honest answers across all three — this cluster converts well when handled generously |

## Quick decision heuristic

Same as Matrix 1: identify category/combo → look up handling → keep reply to
the length the handling allows → end with a nudge or CTA based on message
depth → extract one qualifier if not already captured.
